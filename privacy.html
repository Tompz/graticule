GRATICULE

Privacy

Graticule collects nothing. What it reads stays on your phone.

Effective 28 September 2026. Graticule is made by Dave Thompson, an independent developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • There is no account, and nothing to sign in to.
  • There are no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code that reports anything.
  • There is no Graticule server. Nothing you see in the app is sent to me or to anyone else.

What the app reads, and why

Only what you allow, only on the phone, and only to draw your widgets.

  • Calendar: today's events and the week ahead, for Day and the calendar looks.
  • Reminders: open reminders and their due dates, for Outstanding and similar widgets.
  • Health: step counts and workouts, read only, for Steps and Almanac. Graticule never writes to Health.
  • The phone itself: battery level and free storage, for the Vitals widget.
  • Figures you type in, such as the ones Doomscroll uses.

This is read when a widget is drawn. It is held in memory, or in the app's own private storage on the phone where a widget needs its last reading, and it is never sent anywhere. Health data is never used for advertising and is never shared.

You can change any of these permissions at any time in the Settings app. A widget that loses access says so rather than showing something wrong.

When the app uses the network

  • The App Store, when you buy or restore Graticule Plus. Apple handles the payment; I receive no payment details and no personal information.
  • iCloud Drive, if you keep widgets of your own there. They sync through your own iCloud account, which I cannot see.
  • Addresses named in widgets you add yourself. A widget you import may read a public web address; the app shows you which ones before you save it, and fetches them with nothing attached. If you enter a private address and token, the token is kept in the phone's Keychain and only ever sent to the address it was saved with.

The built-in widgets use none of these. With nothing of your own added, Graticule makes no network requests other than the App Store's.

Children

Graticule is suitable for everyone and collects nothing from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page will change first, with a new date at the top, and the app's App Store privacy label will change with it.
